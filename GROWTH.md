# Algora — Why students come, and how they spread it

> Companion to `INTERACTIVE-PLAN.md`. That file answers *what to build*. This one answers
> *why anyone shows up*, and which build decisions are actually growth decisions.
>
> **This file starts at launch day.** For the weeks *before* launch — the half-open door, the
> screen-recording and Remotion video pipeline, professor outreach, and the six-week countdown —
> see `PRE-GROWTH.md`.

---

## 0. The uncomfortable truth first

Nobody will ever search for "algora". Zero people wake up wanting a "gamified algorithm
learning platform". They wake up wanting **one specific thing they are stuck on tonight.**

So the site does not get discovered as a *brand*. It gets discovered as **the best possible
answer to a narrow question a student typed into Google at 11pm.** Everything below follows
from that single fact.

The failure mode to avoid: a gorgeous product with a login wall in front of it, invisible to
search, that only the founder's friends ever see.

---

## 1. Why they come (the three real triggers)

Students do not arrive "to learn DSA". They arrive in one of three emotional states.

### Trigger A — "I have to understand this by tomorrow" (panic)
**Who:** 2nd/3rd-year CS student, exam or assignment due.
**Query:** *"how does dijkstra's algorithm work step by step"*, *"quicksort partition explained"*.
**What they want:** to *see* it move, once, right now. Not a course. Not an account.
**Why Algora wins:** a textbook gives a static diagram; a YouTube video makes them scrub a
timeline to find second 4:12. Algora lets them **step through it at their own pace with their
own input** — which is exactly the thing neither of the other two can do.
**This is the wedge.** It is also the highest-volume, lowest-competition traffic.

### Trigger B — "I have interviews in 10 weeks" (ambition)
**Who:** final-year student or 1–3 yr engineer grinding for FAANG.
**Query:** *"leetcode study plan"*, *"blind 75 order"*, *"how to prepare DSA in 3 months"*.
**What they want:** a plan they can trust, and to stop feeling behind.
**Why Algora wins:** LeetCode tells you *whether* you were wrong. Algora shows you *why* —
the visualizer plus the roadmap (batch 9) turns a wall of 2,000 problems into a day-by-day path.
**This is the money.** Trigger B pays; Trigger A mostly doesn't.

### Trigger C — "this is genuinely beautiful" (delight)
**Who:** devs on Reddit / X / Hacker News, and teachers.
**What they want:** something satisfying to watch and to forward.
**Why Algora wins:** the animation quality *is* the marketing asset.
**This is the distribution.** Trigger C brings A and B for free.

> **Design consequence:** A needs *speed and zero friction*. B needs *structure and proof of
> progress*. C needs *beauty and a share button*. If a feature serves none of the three, cut it.

## 2. The one rule that makes or breaks acquisition

**Every algorithm page must be fully playable, with no account, indexed by Google.**

`/algorithms/dijkstra` should let a stranger hit play, scrub, change the input, and leave —
never once seeing a signup form. That page is simultaneously:

- your SEO landing page,
- your product demo,
- and the thing people paste into group chats.

Gate *progress* (streaks, roadmap, saved work, review queue) — never gate *understanding*.
A login wall in front of the visualizer would destroy all three functions at once.

**Target:** ~60 algorithm pages = ~60 doors into the site. A brand has one door; a library has sixty.

## 3. Acquisition channels, in the order they actually pay off

| # | Channel | Effort | Why it works for Algora |
|---|---------|--------|--------------------------|
| 1 | **Programmatic SEO** — one page per algorithm/concept | high, one-time | Evergreen, compounding, matches Trigger A intent exactly. Each page: definition, live visualizer, complexity table, dry run, common mistakes, related problems. |
| 2 | **Reddit / X / Hacker News launches** | low | r/learnprogramming, r/leetcode, r/computerscience, r/csMajors. Post a 15-second screen recording of one visualizer — never a landing-page link. Show, don't pitch. |
| 3 | **Teachers / professors** | medium | One lecturer adopting it = 200 students per semester, every semester. The highest-leverage single conversation available to you. Embeds (§4) are what make this possible. |
| 4 | **Short-form video** | medium | Silent 20s loops of a sort animating. The product is inherently visual; this is free content generated by the thing you already built. |
| 5 | **Comparison/long-tail content** | low | *"BFS vs DFS"*, *"when to use a heap"*, *"time complexity cheat sheet"* — each ends in a live demo. |
| 6 | **Campus ambassadors / coding clubs** | medium | Only once retention is proven. Don't start here. |

Paid ads: not yet. Student CAC exceeds student LTV until Trigger B conversion is proven.

## 4. How they show it to other people (build these, they are features)

Sharing must be **a product mechanic, not a social icon**. Five concrete builds:

1. **Shareable state URLs.** Encode the input, algorithm and step into the URL:
   `/algorithms/quicksort?input=5,3,8,1&step=12`. A student sends their friend *the exact frame
   they're confused about*. This is the single highest-value sharing feature — it makes the site
   the medium for explaining, not just a place to read.
2. **Auto-generated share cards.** Canvas-rendered OG image per algorithm (already scoped in
   batch 10) so every pasted link unfurls as a beautiful preview in Discord/WhatsApp/X instead
   of a naked URL. Link previews are most of why a link gets clicked.
3. **Embeds for teachers and bloggers.** `<iframe src="/embed/dijkstra">` plus a copy-embed
   button. Lecture slides and course pages become permanent inbound traffic, and each embed is
   also a backlink.
4. **Achievement cards.** "Finished Graphs · 12-day streak" as a downloadable image (batch 8).
   Students post progress; posts carry the wordmark.
5. **Study-group invites.** A shared roadmap two friends can see. Social obligation is the
   strongest retention force that exists — far stronger than any streak counter.

> Note what all five have in common: the user shares because it **helps them**
> (explaining, teaching, showing off, committing) — not because you asked them to.

## 5. Why they come *back* (acquisition is worthless without this)

Trigger A visitors are one-night stands by default. Convert them with a **soft, earned prompt**:
after they've stepped through a full animation, offer *"save this / get the 30-day graph plan"* —
at the moment of demonstrated value, never on arrival.

Then three return mechanics, in order of real-world power:
1. **The roadmap** (batch 9) — an unfinished plan is a reason to return tomorrow.
2. **The review queue** (batch 6) — spaced repetition creates a *scheduled* reason.
3. **Streaks/XP** (batch 8) — reinforcement, not the foundation. Streaks alone don't retain;
   they only amplify something that's already working.

## 6. First 90 days, concretely

- **Weeks 1–4** — Batches 1–4. Ship **10** flawless algorithm pages, public and indexed.
  Depth over breadth: 10 perfect pages beat 60 mediocre ones for both SEO and word of mouth.
- **Weeks 5–8** — Batch 5 + sitemap, OG images, embeds. Post 3 Reddit demos. Email 20 professors
  with a link to one page relevant to their syllabus — not a pitch deck.
- **Weeks 9–12** — Batches 6, 8, 9. Now retention exists, so turn traffic into accounts.
  Metric that matters: **% of visitors who press play**, then **% who return on day 7**.

Ignore signups as the headline metric until "pressed play" is above ~40%. If strangers won't
press play, nothing downstream can be fixed by more traffic.

---

## 7. One-line positioning

> **LeetCode tells you that you were wrong. Algora shows you why.**

Say that to a student in one breath and they understand the product. If a homepage headline
takes longer than that to land, it's the wrong headline.
